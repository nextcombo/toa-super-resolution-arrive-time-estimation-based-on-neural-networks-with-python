# [TOA] Super-Resolution Arrive Time Estimation Based on Neural Networks [with Python Code]

This paper proposes a learning-based algorithm that estimates the arrival time (ToA) of radio frequency (RF) signals for wireless positioning applications through channel frequency response (CFR) measurements. It introduces a generator neural network to enhance the effective bandwidth of narrowband CFR measurements and generate high-resolution channel impulse response (CIR) estimates. Additionally, two regression neural networks are introduced based on the enhanced CIR for two-step coarse-to-fine ToA estimation. Compared to traditional super-resolution algorithms in simulated channels, the proposed method improves the root mean square error (RMSE) of distance ranging by 9% - 58%, and the false alarm rate is improved by up to 22%. In real measurement channels, this method improves by 1.3 meters at a 90th percentile distance error.
In radio frequency-based positioning, the goal of ToA estimation is to find the shortest propagation delay from the transmitter to the receiver, which is proportional to the distance. The theoretical precision of ToA estimation is fundamentally limited by the bandwidth of the radio frequency signal. In actual systems, the bandwidth is severely limited due to interference from other devices, complexity of transceivers, and system power consumption [1][2]. When the bandwidth is constrained to several tens of megahertz within ISM bands, the Nyquist sampled channel impulse response (CIR) can only provide very coarse (hundreds of meters) resolution.
Inspired by the recent significant success of deep learning, we propose a two-stage, deep learning-based algorithm that involves multiple neural networks to estimate ToA with higher resolution. Firstly, we train a generator network that: 1) expands the effective bandwidth and generates higher resolution CIR; 2) performs denoising to distinguish between actual pulses in the CIR and noise. In the subsequent stage, we apply a coarse-to-fine cascade estimation process, first inputting the high-resolution CIR generated by the generator network into a regressor network to obtain a rough estimate of ToA. Then, we train another regressor network that focuses only on the interested areas around the rough estimate of ToA to estimate ToA more precisely. In the experimental section, we show that our proposed method significantly outperforms traditional methods in both simulated and real-world channel measurements.

Conclusion In this paper, we propose a ToA estimation algorithm based on deep learning that utilizes an up-resolution generator to expand the effective bandwidth of ToA estimation. We introduce a two-stage training and estimation process, which achieves significant gains over traditional and other neural network-based ToA estimation algorithms. The proposed method is evaluated through real CIR measurements, demonstrating its ability to reliably transfer from simulated training data to actual channels.
